Run 2026-10-11. Definitions, thresholds and pass rules were written in `docs/validation_protocol.md` **before** any T10 data was pulled (sha256 of the original file `751cc8cf...2250b85a`; Amendment 1, added before the E analysis, brings it to `c13aa645...2a273301b1`). No test was re-run with changed thresholds. All results are **hindcast** (NWM v3.0 retrospective, free-running, no data assimilation); none is forecast skill and none is a closure probability. Report labels are **provisional: my suggested decisions, not yet reviewed by you** (`reviewer_decision` overrides when you fill it; the script reruns in about a minute, the 1979-2023 records are cached).

**What these results license.** The layer is good at *when*: at the hours people report flooded roads, the NWM flow on the crossing's reach is almost always in the top percentile of that reach's 44-year record, it peaks within a day, and the flow matches gauges. It is **not shown to be good at *which crossings* close**: the earlier cross-crossing ranking (phase 1, p = 0.58 / 0.78 and every alternative rule in `data/phase2/t2a_rule_comparison.csv`, best p = 0.14) and the E1 failure agree. The q10 closure rule also has **low sensitivity** (below), and the model does **not** reproduce the documented isolation of Wilmington.

## A. Report set (provisional, unreviewed)
IEM Local Storm Reports, NC, 2016-01-01..2022-12-31: 38,674 rows -> 23,605 unique NC reports -> **709** flood-type closure-wording road reports (tree/power-line and surge/tide wording excluded) -> 213 with a statewide seed structure (NCDOT bridge/culvert/pipe + NBI, waterway-like, 16,135 structures) on a road the remark names within 1 km -> **120 within 500 m** -> 113 with an assessed NWM reach (reach within 100 m, q2 >= 1 cfs) -> **74 suggested-accept** (66 crossings, 66 reaches, 39 counties, 24 storm clusters; by year 2017: 7, 2018: 36, 2019: 10, 2020: 16, 2021: 1, 2022: 4) and 15 uncertain. **No 2016 (Matthew) report survived** (zero in 2016). I read all 120 and corrected the automatic suggestion in 29 cases (automatic rule over-rejects when the closed road follows "and/on/at", and it accepted one landmark): file `data/phase2/t10/report_candidates.csv` (`suggested_decision`, `suggested_reason`, `auto_suggested_decision`; reviewer columns blank). Weaknesses: report points are rounded to ~1 km; report time is not onset time; road matching uses structure road names plus OSM names within 30 m, so reports naming an SR road by its local name are often missed (recall is low, precision is the aim); 2018 is half the set.

## B. Flow percentile test (primary, fixed in advance)
For each report: NWM v3.0 retro flow on its reach at the report hour; percentile in the reach's own 1979-02-01..2023-01-31 hourly record (385,704 hours, no missing); ratios to the OWP q2/q10 (the only thresholds); 200 controls per report (same reach, same calendar month, >72 h from any candidate report on that reach); matched AUC; bootstrap over storm clusters (gap > 48 h), 10,000 resamples.

| | Reports (74) | Controls (200 each) |
|---|---|---|
| median percentile in own record | **0.9996** | 0.437 |
| share with flow > q2 | 60.8% (45/74) | 0.73% |
| share with flow > q10 | 28.4% (21/74) | 0.07% |
| median flow / q2, flow / q10 | 1.29, 0.43 | - |

AUC **0.958**, 95% CI [0.916, 0.992]; difference in share > q2 **+0.601** [0.398, 0.726]; difference > q10 +0.283 [0.087, 0.406]. Pre-declared rule (AUC lower bound > 0.5 and q2-share difference lower bound > 0): **PASS**. Secondaries (do not change the verdict): window max +-6 h: AUC 0.981 [0.945, 0.999], > q2 82.4%, > q10 44.6%; order >= 2 only (57 reports): AUC 0.962 [0.915, 0.996]; accept + uncertain (89): AUC 0.963 [0.934, 0.990]; leave-one-cluster-out AUC 0.954-0.973. By order: AUC 0.92-1.0 in every stream order, so order-1 reaches are not worse here.

**Limits of this test.** Controls are ordinary hours of the same calendar month, so the contrast is "storm hour vs random hour". It shows the flow responds to storms when closures are reported; it does not show the layer separates a flooded crossing from a dry one in the same storm. That harder test is the cross-crossing ranking, which failed (phase 1 and `t2a`).
**Descriptive, post hoc (not part of the verdict):** as a closure *rule*, flow > q10 at the report hour catches only 28% of reports (45% within +-6 h), flow > q2 catches 61% (82%); false alarms at control hours are 0.07% and 0.7%. So RL10 under-detects reported closures, RL2 detects more but saturates (phase 1: 217/250 crossings above q2 at the first forecast hour).

## C. Timing test
Window +-72 h; hit = hour of maximum flow within +-24 h of the report. Reports **81.1%** hit vs controls **14.0%** (chance level for this window); difference **+0.671** [0.556, 0.841]; restricted to windows whose max exceeds q2: +0.587 [0.476, 0.760]. **PASS.** (Reports are positives selected at storm peaks, so the chance level is the right comparison, not 33%.)

## D. Gauge validation (T9), 43 pilot gauges
Pilot-flag crosswalk gauges with observed hourly discharge: 43 of 46. Events Matthew (2016-10-05..25) and Florence (2018-09-10..10-31); a gauge-event is scored with >= 80% observed coverage: **73 of 86 scored** (Matthew 34, Florence 39). NWM retro vs observed discharge: median NSE 0.55, median KGE 0.41, 84% of gauge-events NSE > 0; **peak ratio between 0.5 and 2.0 in 76.7%**; median peak ratio 0.98 (Florence 1.25, Matthew 0.80); **median absolute peak-time error 4 h**. Exceedance of q2: 3,689 hours both, 2,351 observed only, 1,539 NWM only (hit rate 61.1%, precision 70.6%). Pass rule (>= 70% within 0.5-2.0 and median |timing| <= 12 h): **PASS**. NSE/KGE are not part of the rule. Gauges here are on larger, gauged streams than most crossings; this says nothing about small ungauged order-1 reaches.

## E. Documented Florence closures
`docs/florence_documented_closures.csv`: 87 rows, **85 verified** (source fetched, quote present; NCDOT and Newsweek quotes were also string-checked against the raw page HTML saved in `data/phase2/t10/src_pages`; NWS and ABC11 text is from the fetch tool, curl was blocked) and **2 unverified** (search summary only). Sources: NCDOT releases 2018-09-19/22/24, NWS Wilmington Florence page, Newsweek 2018-09-21, ABC11 2018-09-25. Almost everything is **date-level only**; the one snapshot is NCDOT's "about 3 p.m. Wednesday, Sept. 19, about 800 sections of road were closed" by county and road. No closure/reopen *times* exist for most roads (hence the records request in `docs/records_requests.md`).

- **E1 (snapshot, 19:00 UTC 2018-09-19; model closed = flow > q10):** 116 (road, county) pairs with an assessed statewide structure: 70 NCDOT-listed, 46 not listed (same 11 counties, I-/US-/NC- roads). Listed: 55 hit (78.6%); unlisted: 30 (65.2%); one-sided Fisher p = 0.085. **FAIL** both parts of the rule (needs >= 60% and p < 0.05; the share passes, the contrast does not). Sensitivity: q2 92.9% vs 89.1% (p = 0.35); q50 38.6% vs 21.7% (p = 0.043 but 38.6% < 60%). Why the contrast is weak: nearly everything was model-closed during Florence (98% of pairs were model-closed at some hour 09-14..09-30, and none in the 27 Aug - 9 Sep pre-period, so the layer is quiet in normal weather), and the NCDOT list is "sections", not exhaustive, which biases toward failing.
- **E2 (dated closures):** I-40 Duplin/Pender/New Hanover (09-15..09-24), I-95 Robeson/Cumberland/Harnett (09-16..09-23), US-17 and NC-87 Brunswick (09-14/15): **8 of 8 units hit, 8 of 8 timing hits** (first model-closed hour on 09-14 12:00 to 09-16 19:00). Rule passes, but with a saturated layer this proves little: any road would pass. **Not evidence of validation.**
- **E3 (graph vs documented isolation):** NCDOT/NWS say every route into Wilmington (I-40, US-17, 74, 76, 421) was closed and officials said on Sept 20 there was no safe route. Imposing the documented closures on the phase-1b graph at the snapshot: with **every** statewide structure on an NCDOT-listed road/county closed (326 segments, an upper bound because the closed "sections" are not located), loss of access to the derived exit points is Brunswick 12.7%, Pender 0.2%, **Wilmington/south 1.3%**, New Hanover north 0%; to ED hospitals Brunswick 9.7%, Wilmington/south 1.3%. With only the structures the model closes at that hour (q10, 137 segments): **0% everywhere**, although NCDOT lists six Brunswick roads closed that day (the model's Brunswick exit loss at RL10 is gone by 09-16 19:00 and at RL2 by 09-19 08:00, so the model recedes before documented closures end). Reasons Wilmington is not reproduced: exits are at the three-county boundary, so closures inland (I-40 in Duplin, NC-24, US-74/76 in Columbus) cannot register; only waterway structures can close; and the road grid around Wilmington has many alternates. **The graph was not extended inland** (I-40/I-95/US-74/76 corridors); that needs a larger graph and inland exit/destination set and was not done in this pass.
- **Exit-loss pattern vs documentation** (phase 1b, seed, RL10 first >= 1%): Brunswick from 09-14 14:00 (documented US-17/NC-87 closures 09-14/15: **consistent in onset**), Pender from 09-15 16:00 (documented I-40 flooded 09-15: **consistent**), Wilmington none (documented isolation: **miss**). Duration: model Brunswick ends 09-16 (RL10) vs documented closures through at least 09-19..09-24: **miss**.

## F. Routing validation vs OSRM
150 origin blocks (50 per county, seed 20261011), engine baseline minutes to the nearest ED hospital vs OSRM public demo server (policy read at github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/wiki/Demo-server: <= 1 request/s, non-commercial; we sent 150 requests at ~1.2 s spacing; `src/36_t10_routing_check.py`). MAE **3.22 min** (mean OSRM 19.4 min), median absolute % error **15.5%**, 91.3% of pairs within +-30%, bias **-2.7 min** (engine faster than OSRM). Counties: Brunswick MAE 2.4 min, New Hanover 2.4, Pender 4.8. Rule (median APE <= 20% and >= 90% within +-30%): **PASS**, with little margin on the second part. Worst outlier: a Pender block, engine 24.4 vs OSRM 91.9 min (-67.5): origin 1.3 km from the graph in the engine and 0.8 km in OSRM, so the two snapped to different roads (an origin-snapping artifact, not a path-time comparison); the next worst are -11.2 and -9.3 min. Engine drive times are systematically a little optimistic (default OSM speeds, no turn/intersection delay).

## T12 High-water marks vs lidar road crest
- **Source (verified):** USGS STN Flood Event Viewer services. Event list `https://stn.wim.usgs.gov/STNServices/Events.json` gives `event_id 283, "2018 Florence"` (2018-09-07..10-07). HWMs from `https://stn.wim.usgs.gov/STNServices/HWMs/FilteredHWMs.json?Event=283&States=NC`: **462** NC HWMs; 417 qualify (NAVD88 with elevation; "VP" and "Unknown" quality dropped): 356 riverine, 61 coastal. Raw JSON saved: `data/phase2/t12/stn_hwms_florence_nc.json`.
- **Datum:** HWM `elev_ft` is NAVD88 feet (`verticalDatumName`), converted x 0.3048; 3DEP 1 m DEM is NAVD88 metres. No datum shift needed; 28 non-NAVD88 or blank-datum HWMs were excluded, not converted.
- **Pairing and exclusions:** 346 statewide seed crossings have a qualifying HWM within 1 km, 151 within 300 m. Of the 151: 76 bridges >= 35 m (excluded as long/high-level, cutoff from phase 0's 14 NGS marks, n = 14), 44 short bridges, 29 culverts/pipes, 2 bridges of unknown length. Crest obtained for 282 of 346 (42 no 1 m tile, 18 tile no-data, 4 no named road within 30 m; none filled). Primary set: 73 crossings, 54 with crest, 47 also with an assessed NWM reach.

Per county (above/with crest): Robeson 5/9, Duplin 5/5, Onslow 4/7, Johnston 3/4, Beaufort 3/3, New Hanover 3/4, Wayne 2/6, Craven 2/4, Bladen 2/3, Sampson 2/2, Lenoir 0/2, Pender 1/2, Jones 1/1, Pamlico 1/1, Richmond 1/1 (Brunswick: none in the primary set).
- **Model at those crossings (hindcast v3.0, peak 09-07..10-07):** median peak/q10 **5.41** where HWM > crest vs **3.24** where HWM < crest; AUC 0.55 [0.37, 0.73] -> **FAIL**. Peak/q2: 23.1 vs 11.5, AUC 0.56 [0.38, 0.74]. Even crossings whose nearby HWM stayed below the crest had modeled peaks ~3x their 10-year flow: the saturation problem again.
- **Secondaries (do not change the verdict):** HWM <= 1 km: 122 with crest, 60.7% above; AUC 0.63 [0.51, 0.75], nominally above 0.5 but one of four secondaries, so not a pass; including unknown-length bridges: AUC 0.52 [0.34, 0.70]; coastal HWMs: 8 of 10 above crest, too few with a reach to test.
- **Limits:** the HWM is up to 300 m (1 km) from the crossing, with no water-surface slope correction; the crest is the max DEM on the OSM road +-40 m from the 2020-2025 lidar (roads may have changed since 2018); DEM-vs-survey error ~1 m (phase 0, n = 14).

## Result (primary, pre-declared): pooled mean of event AUCs, Helene excluded, peak/q10

## What this changes
- This is the **first crossing-level test that passes its pre-registered rule**: on 2023-2025 storms, crossings next to NCDOT-recorded flood closures had higher modeled peak flow ratios than other crossings in the same counties more often than not (pooled mean event AUC 0.72, CI 0.66-0.75), with large variation by event (0.50-0.82) and weaker discrimination in Helene.
- It is still **not a closure predictor**: precision at q10 ~5%, and flows from the operational analysis (v2.2/v3.0, with data assimilation) differ from the retrospective used elsewhere.
- Allowed wording: "In six 2023-2025 NC storm windows, crossings near recorded flood closures had higher modeled flow ratios than other crossings in the same counties (AUC 0.72 [0.66, 0.75], excluding Helene; operational analysis; recorded closures only)." Not allowed: forecast skill, that the layer predicts closures, or applies equally to Helene/mountain terrain.

## Post-hoc diagnostic (NOT pre-registered; does not change the verdict) - where does the gain come from?
Same HGB settings and LOSO scheme, different feature sets (`t16_posthoc_feature_families.csv`): **flow features only** (log peak/q10, log peak/q2, hours above q2): pooled 0.663 (Helene 0.637); **structure features only, no flow at all** (length, stream order, tidal, type): **0.716** (Helene 0.705); stream order alone 0.667; full model 0.798. So the two families together beat either alone, but **structure attributes without any flood information rank closure-positive crossings better than the flow features do**, and better than the raw peak/q10 baseline (0.690).

**Reading.** The pre-registered pass is real but it is not evidence that the model understands flood hazard. Stream order and structure length (larger streams and longer structures sit on state-maintained roads that NCDOT records) most plausibly act as proxies for how likely a closure is to be recorded and matched, the same reporting bias that already weakens T15. What can be said: within these nine storm windows, a model that combines hindcast flow with basic structure attributes ranks recorded-closure crossings better than peak flow ratio alone (pooled 0.798 vs 0.690; within-county 0.708 vs 0.626). What cannot: that it predicts closures, that it works as a forecast (the features use the whole event), or that the improvement is hydrological. No forecast skill; negatives include unrecorded closures.
