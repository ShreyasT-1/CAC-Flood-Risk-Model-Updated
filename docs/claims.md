## 2. CLAIMS WE DO NOT MAKE (and why)

1. **"FloodLens predicts which roads will close."** The crossing-level tests are weak or failed: phase-1 ranking of reported vs unreported crossings (p = 0.58 / 0.78; no alternative rule better than p = 0.14, `data/phase2/t2a_rule_comparison.csv`), E1 (NCDOT list vs unlisted, p = 0.085), T12 and T13 (below). The one pass (C5) is a pooled ranking result with precision about 5%, not a closure predictor.
2. **Forecast skill.** Every validation test is a hindcast or an operational-analysis replay. The one forecast replay (v1.2 short-range, starting 2018-09-17) cannot show onset (217 of 250 crossings already above q2 at its first hour) and was not scored for skill. No lead-time claim.
3. **"Closure labels are validated."** Labels are my suggested decisions on 120 candidates (74 accept / 15 uncertain), **not reviewed by the project owner**; coordinates are rounded to about 1 km; the Florence label file has not been confirmed (`data/phase2/t10/report_candidates.csv`). No claim "validated against Florence closures": E1 failed, E2 passed but cannot discriminate (saturation).
4. **Any "safe", "no risk", "protected" or "will stay open" statement** about a road, block or address. The closure scenarios see river flow only (no surge, tide, pluvial/drainage flooding, deck height, scour, debris; the separate coastal NOWCAST is not combined with them); a crossing that is not flagged is "not flagged in this scenario", never an assurance.
5. **"Wilmington loses no access."** Under the phase-1 engine Wilmington/south showed 0% loss, but that reflects the crossing inventory and graph (exits at the three-county boundary, only waterway structures can close, many alternate roads), not Florence. NCDOT/NWS documented every route into Wilmington as closed. Test E3: even closing every structure on every NCDOT-listed road gives only 1.3% exit loss in Wilmington/south. The model does not reproduce Wilmington's documented isolation; the graph was not extended inland.
6. **Any ML forecast skill, or ML skill from NWM data alone.** An untuned HGB on NWM-only features did not beat `baseline_ratio` on held-out events and unseen gauges (`reports/sample_model.md`). The only ML claim (C8) is a nowcast that needs the gauge's own observed stage; it is not a forecast and says nothing about which roads close.
7. **That a multi-feature model (T16) is a hazard or closure model.** It passed its registered rule but its gain is largely carried by structure attributes (see the failed-tests table); we do not describe it as understanding flood hazard.
8. **That the recurrence levels (RL2...RL50) are closure probabilities or calibrated.** They are flow levels; official flood stage is a median 3.6x the NWM high-water threshold (`reports/phase1_limitations.md` item 4). The "17c" suffix of the recurrence-flow fields is unverified.
9. **That the exported "address report" is address-level.** It is a Census block centroid estimate, stamped `SAMPLE - block-centroid estimate`; there are no individual addresses.

## 4. DATA-SOURCE CAVEATS (state with every number that uses them)

- **Operational NWM with data assimilation (T15).** Events after 2023-02-01 use NWM operational analysis files from NOAA's public Google Cloud mirror `gs://national-water-model` (the S3 bucket `noaa-nwm-pds` only holds dates from 2025-01-01). Different forcing and USGS assimilation from the v3.0 retrospective used in T10, T12 and T13, and the recurrence thresholds' provenance is unverified, so ratios are not directly comparable across tests.
- **E1 (Aug 2023) is on NWM v2.2; E2-E9 are on v3.0.** (`data/phase2/t15/t15_flow_meta.json`.)
- **Storm names verified only for Debby (E3, AL04) and Helene (E5).** E1, E2, E4 and E6-E9 are identified by date windows from NCDOT closure records only; names were not verified.
- **Label reporting bias.** Positives are NCDOT-recorded closures within 100 m of an inventoried structure (state-maintained and busy roads over-represented); negatives include closures that were never recorded. Both push AUC down, and "precision" is not literally interpretable.
- **T15 event selection.** The six-event cap was pre-registered (Amendments 3 and 4); the primary-test wording ("qualifying events") is loose, so both the six-event (0.716) and nine-event (0.690) results are shown (`reports/phase2_validation.md`, follow-up section).
- Hindcast flows are free-running; 2018 tropical-storm timing and magnitude errors are known (limitations item 8). Report coordinates are rounded to about 1 km; closures reported are positives only (limitations items 10-11).
- **Origin snap.** Blocks are snapped to the nearest graph node (phase-1 snap). A phase-2 re-snap (to the largest connected network) was checked on 2026-10-12 and rejected: 10 of 30 sampled blocks were snapped to service-only roads, and the 21 blocks it dropped (578 people) had to be removed (14 of them would cross water to reach the main network). 88 blocks (6,921 people) therefore stay baseline-unreachable and are "not assessed" in every loss count; the two snaps differ by 1.1% in the Florence RL2 peak count of people losing hospital access (`docs/export_snap_sidebyside.md`, `data/phase2/resnap_check/resnap_check.csv`).
- **FEMA NFHL.** Zone assigned by point-in-polygon from the public service as served on 2026-10-12 (no dataset-vintage field); polygons overlap in places (highest hazard used); a comparison layer only.
- **Live layer (`data/export/live_conditions.json/.csv`).** A nowcast / scenario display of NOAA operational National Water Model output (current flow vs each reach's recurrence flows, with its cycle time and age); it adds **no new validation claim** and is not a closure forecast.
- **Four modes, never mixed (added 2026-10-14; no new validation claim, no new pre-registered test: nothing here was compared with outcomes).** REPLAY = Florence hindcast (`export/sample/crossings.json` etc.). NOWCAST = observed now: `export/sample/nowcast.json` (USGS Water Data API discharge/gage height + NWS NWPS stage and NWS flood category at the 46 pilot gauges) and `export/sample/coastal.json` (NOAA CO-OPS observed vs predicted water level at Wilmington 8658120 [riverine-influenced], Wrightsville Beach 8658163; Southport 8659084 predictions only). FORECAST = `export/sample/forecast_latest.json`: latest complete NOAA NWM short-range cycle (18 leads) for the 32 sample reaches, flow / q10, "scenario: flagged" when flow > q10; labelled "NWM short-range forecast, unvalidated; river flow only"; **not a closure probability, no skill claimed**, not an input to any ML placeholder value. OUTLOOK-PERSISTENCE = the coastal outlook that holds the latest residual constant (`synthetic: true`), a placeholder, not surge guidance. Every record carries a UTC time; nowcast records carry `stale` (> 3 h old); the forecast carries cycle age (`stale` > 6 h) and a `fallback` block when the newest cycle was incomplete.
- **What is and is not in the tide/surge layer.** Included: observed water level, astronomical tide prediction and their difference (a nowcast anomaly, not a surge forecast) at three stations, in NAVD88 metres. Still excluded: wave run-up, rain/drainage flooding, river-surge interaction, any official NHC/NOS surge guidance; the water level is **not** combined with crossings or closure scenarios, so the REPLAY and RL10 scenarios remain river-flow-only. Wilmington's residual includes Cape Fear River flow.
- **Gauge status caveat.** USGS gage height and NWS stage are on different datums and are never compared; the flood status shown is NWS's own category for NWS's stage. A gauge below flood stage is not an all-clear for any road.
- **FORECAST data caveats.** The hourly archive (`data/forecasts`, branch `nwm-archive` on GitHub) holds only the 256 gauge reaches; just 1 of the 32 sample reaches is in it, so `src/44_forecast_preview.py` fetches the sample reaches directly from the NOAA operational files. q10 flows are the same recurrence flows as the replay (provenance unverified, claims section 2 item 8); the forecast comes from the current operational NWM (v3.x, data-assimilating) while the replay uses the v3.0 retrospective, so ratios are not directly comparable. The 2018 archive cycles are replays of NWM v1.2 and are never shown as forecasts.

## 5. DRAFT WORDING

**App text (3 sentences).** FloodLens Lifelines shows scenarios for how flooding on rivers and creeks could cut off road crossings, and which neighborhoods could lose their route to a hospital or fire station if a given crossing closed. The scenarios use National Water Model river-flow data compared with each stream's own flood-flow levels; they do not include storm surge, tides, drainage flooding or bridge height, and they are not forecasts of what will happen. Past-storm checks show the flow data tracks when flooding is happening, but they do not show which individual roads will close, so treat every map as one possible scenario.

**CAC written answer (about 60 words).** FloodLens Lifelines turns National Water Model river-flow data into scenarios of which road crossings could close and which neighborhoods could lose routes to hospitals and fire stations. In past-storm tests, river flow was unusually high when closures were reported, and crossings near recorded closures ranked higher. It does not predict closures; every result is a scenario.

**Video, 30 seconds spoken (about 75 words).** When a river rises, a bridge can close and a whole neighborhood can lose its way to a hospital. FloodLens Lifelines uses National Water Model flow data to show scenarios of which crossings might close, and who would be cut off if they did. We tested it on past storms: it follows when flooding happens, but it does not tell us which exact roads close. So it shows possibilities, not promises.
